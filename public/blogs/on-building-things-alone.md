# On Building Things Alone

There's a specific kind of quiet that happens at 2am when you're the only person awake and your code is finally doing what you imagined it would do three weeks ago.

I've been trying to describe it to people. The closest I've gotten is: it feels like the room gets a little warmer.

---

I've been building solo projects for about four years now. Not as a productivity flex, not because I prefer it — I've just ended up here, often. A lot of what I make starts as a thing I needed and couldn't find, or a feeling I wanted to understand by making it concrete.

The hard part isn't the code. The hard part is the middle.

Every project has a middle. It's the phase after the excitement of the idea but before the satisfaction of the thing working. The middle is where you realize your architecture was wrong, where the feature you thought would take an afternoon takes a week, where you open the file and can't remember what you were trying to do.

Most things die in the middle.

---

What I've noticed about the projects that make it out: they were all things I genuinely needed to exist. Not "this would be a cool portfolio piece" — those always stall. But "I keep wishing this existed and it doesn't, so I guess I have to make it."

There's something about necessity that pulls you through the middle. You're not building for the imagined user. You're building for the version of yourself that needed it last Tuesday.

---

I don't have a productivity system. I don't do the Pomodoro technique or track deep work hours. I just try to remember what I was excited about before the middle started.

Sometimes I write it down on a sticky note and put it on my monitor.

*I'm building this because...*

It sounds corny. It works.

---

The room-getting-warmer feeling at 2am — I think it's just relief. Relief that the middle is over. Relief that the thing you imagined is now a thing that exists.

It's a small feeling. But I keep chasing it.
